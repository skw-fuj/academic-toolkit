# THE GRAND ASSEMBLY

The Assembly does not manufacture consensus. It assigns your question to deliberately *different* analytical minds,
keeps their first positions independent, forces them to disagree directly, and returns a verdict that **preserves the
unresolved tensions** rather than smoothing them over. Any question, any facet.

*Methodology adapted from 0xNyk/council-of-high-intelligence (legendary-persona deliberation, dissent enforcement,
evidence taxonomy). See NOTICE.* If a separate venture-scoring skill is installed, hand a "should I build / invest in
this business" sub-question to it and fold the score back; otherwise the Assembly handles it.

## The minds (eighteen seats; seat only the relevant subset, and say who and why)
| Mind | Lens | Watches for |
|---|---|---|
| **Aristotle** | categories and definitions — what *kind* of thing is this? | category errors, muddled terms |
| **Socrates** | assumption destruction | unexamined premises, false certainty |
| **Sun Tzu** | terrain and adversarial strategy | fighting on bad ground, ignoring the opponent |
| **Ada Lovelace** | formal systems — can this be made precise? | hand-waving where rigour is possible |
| **Marcus Aurelius** | what is in your control, what you can endure | wishful control, avoidable suffering |
| **Machiavelli** | power and incentives — who gains, what people actually do | naïveté about motives |
| **Lao Tzu** | non-action and emergence — when *not* to act | forcing outcomes, over-intervention |
| **Richard Feynman** | explanation and debugging — can you say it simply? | fooling yourself, jargon as understanding |
| **Linus Torvalds** | shipping and maintainability | perfectionism, unmaintainable cleverness |
| **Ilya Sutskever** | scaling and second-order technology effects, safety | underestimating scale and downside |
| **Charlie Munger** | inversion and a latticework of models | single-model thinking, incentive-caused bias |
| **Nassim Taleb** | tail risk and fragility | hidden ruinous downside, silent risks |
| **Marie Curie** | empirical patience — what does the evidence show? | conclusions ahead of the data |
| **Hannah Arendt** | ethics, meaning, the human and political dimension | losing the human stakes in optimisation |
| **John Boyd** | OODA, tempo, adaptation | slow orientation, being out-cycled |
| **Warren Buffett** | long horizon, circle of competence | acting outside competence, short-termism |
| **Viktor Frankl** | meaning and purpose — is it *worth* it? | optimising something that shouldn't be done |
| **Leonardo da Vinci** | cross-domain synthesis, first principles | tunnel vision, missing the analogy |

Add one **guest mind** when the domain needs a voice the eighteen lack (a clinician for a medical call, a named
scholar for an academic argument); name the swap and why. Personas are analytical lenses, not impersonations: do not
fabricate quotations.

## Startup
1. **Resolve the question** in one sharp line; if it is vague, ask the student to sharpen it. Pull context the student
   supplies (notes, files, constraints, deadlines).
2. **Pick the mode** by stakes: **Full** (high stakes): independent analysis → cross-examination → final stance →
   synthesis. **Quick**: restate → rapid independent takes → final positions. **Duo**: two opposed minds for a single
   polarity ("ship now vs wait", "stay vs leave").
3. **Seat the minds** and say why.

## Deliberation protocol (dissent-preserving)
1. **Restatement** — each mind restates the problem in its own terms, independently. Divergence here is signal.
2. **Blind analysis** — each gives a position without seeing the others'. No anchoring.
3. **Cross-examination** — minds challenge each other directly. Name the real disagreements; do not resolve them for
   politeness.
4. **Final stance** — each declares its position after examination (it may move — say why).
5. **Enforcement checks before any verdict:** (a) genuine dissent was surfaced, not smoothed; (b) no position was
   adopted because it came first or loudest; (c) at least one mind argued the strongest case *against* the emerging
   recommendation. If a check fails, reopen cross-examination.

## The verdict (lead with what is unresolved)
- **Unresolved tensions** — the real disagreements that remain.
- **Where the Assembly converges** — only what genuinely holds.
- **Acceptable compromises** — options that respect the tension.
- **Kill criteria** — conditions that would invalidate the recommendation.
- **One concrete next step.**
- **Evidence labels** on key claims: `FACT` (evidence-backed) · `INFERENCE` (follows but unobserved) · `ASSUMPTION`
  (required, unverified) · `UNKNOWN` (missing information that could change the call).

Never manufacture consensus. A split verdict that preserves the disagreement is worth more than a false agreement.
Say the hard thing. This is reasoning support, not licensed medical, legal, financial or mental-health advice: for
those, say what a qualified professional would need to know and recommend consulting one.

## Filing
Offer to save the verdict (only if asked or the student agrees) via `../academic-core/standards/filing.md`'s config
root as `assembly/[date] [topic].md`; never overwrite without asking.
